## Project Overview

This project analyzes reported human trafficking victims between **2003 and 2023**, using data to explore the scale, demographic distribution, geographical patterns, and changes in reported victim numbers over time.

The Victim Dashboard was developed to make these patterns easier to explore and understand through interactive visualizations.

Rather than looking only at the total number of victims, the analysis focuses on **who was affected, where victims were reported, how victim numbers changed over time, and how patterns differed across regions**.

---

## Problem Statement

Human trafficking affects people across different countries, genders, and age groups, but the patterns are not evenly distributed.

A major challenge when working with a large trafficking dataset is turning raw figures into meaningful insights that can answer questions such as:

- How have reported victim numbers changed over time?
- Which countries recorded the highest number of victims?
- Are females or males more represented in the reported data?
- How does the distribution of adult and child victims differ across regions?

The dashboard was designed to provide a clearer visual answer to these questions.

---

## Key Objectives

The analysis aimed to:

- Examine victim trends across the available years.
- Identify countries with the highest reported victim figures.
- Analyze victim distribution by gender.
- Compare adult and child victims across regions.
- Identify geographical patterns in reported trafficking victims.
- Present the findings through an interactive and easy-to-understand dashboard.

---

## Data Summary

The dataset covers reported human trafficking activity from **2003 to 2023**.

For the victim analysis, the dashboard presents:

- **245,434 detected victims**
- Victim figures by country
- Victim trends by year
- Gender distribution
- Adult and child victims by region
- Geographical distribution of victims

The dashboard identifies **Pakistan** as the country with the highest reported victim figure and **2019** as the year with the highest number of detected victims in the dashboard.

## Dashboard Overview

The Victim Dashboard was structured around several key analytical views.

### 1. Victim Trends by Year

This visualization tracks how reported victim numbers changed over time.

The trend shows relatively lower and more stable figures during the earlier period, followed by noticeable increases in later years.

A particularly significant increase appears around **2018–2019**, with **2019 recording the highest number of detected victims** shown in the dashboard.

This pattern provides an important starting point for investigating changes in detection, reporting, or trafficking activity during that period.

---

### 2. Victims by Country

The geographical analysis highlights differences in reported victim numbers across countries.

**Pakistan recorded the highest number of detected victims at 59,777**, followed by India with **31,030** reported cases.

Other countries appearing among the most affected include:

- United States
- Philippines
- Romania

The country-level comparison demonstrates that reported trafficking victims are not evenly distributed geographically.

---

### 3. Victims by Gender

The gender analysis shows that women and girls represented the larger proportion of reported victims.

- **Female: 66.03%**
- **Male: 33.97%**

This indicates that approximately two-thirds of the detected victims in the dataset were female.

However, the male figure remains significant and reinforces the importance of ensuring that trafficking prevention, detection, support, and rehabilitation efforts do not overlook male victims.

---

### 4. Adult and Child Victims by Region

The dashboard also compares adult and child victims across major regions.

The distribution varies considerably by region.

Africa recorded a particularly high proportion of child victims, while regions such as Asia, Europe, and the Americas showed a larger share of adult victims.

These differences highlight the importance of considering **regional context and victim demographics** when designing interventions.

---

### 5. Victims by Geography

The geographical visualization provides a global view of reported victim distribution.

It allows users to quickly identify countries with higher reported victim figures and understand the geographical spread of trafficking cases represented in the dataset.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Victim numbers increased significantly around 2018–2019

The yearly trend shows a major increase in reported victims around 2018 and 2019, with 2019 representing the peak year in the dashboard.

This increase should be interpreted carefully because higher reported figures may reflect changes in detection and reporting systems as well as changes in trafficking activity.

### 2. Females represented the majority of reported victims

Women and girls accounted for **66.03%** of detected victims, compared with **33.97%** for males.

This highlights a substantial gender difference within the reported data.

### 3. South Asia shows significant victim figures

Pakistan and India recorded the two highest country-level victim figures in the dashboard, placing South Asia among the most prominent areas in the dataset.

### 4. Child victim patterns differ across regions

The regional comparison demonstrates that children represent a particularly important proportion of reported victims in some regions, especially Africa.

This suggests that anti-trafficking strategies may need to be adapted to the demographic patterns observed within specific regions.

---

## Recommendations

Based on the patterns identified in the analysis:

### Strengthen Victim Identification

Improving detection and reporting systems can help identify victims who may otherwise remain outside official statistics.

### Strengthen Child Protection

Regions showing higher proportions of child victims should receive stronger child protection, monitoring, and prevention interventions.

### Maintain Gender-Specific Support

While females represent the majority of reported victims, male victims should also remain part of trafficking prevention and victim-support strategies.

### Improve Data Collection

Greater consistency in how countries collect and report trafficking data would improve the reliability of international comparisons.

### Use Regional Insights for Targeted Intervention

The differences between regions demonstrate why trafficking interventions should be informed by local patterns rather than relying entirely on a single global approach.

## Limitations

The findings should be interpreted as **reported or detected activity within the dataset**, rather than a definitive measure of the true global prevalence of human trafficking.

Human trafficking is significantly underreported, and differences in national reporting systems, detection capacity, definitions, and data quality can influence the figures recorded.

Therefore, a higher number of reported victims does not necessarily mean that trafficking occurs more frequently in that country. It may also indicate stronger detection or reporting mechanisms.

---

## Conclusion

The Victim Dashboard demonstrates how data analysis can help transform complex trafficking records into meaningful demographic and geographical insights.

The analysis highlights three important dimensions of the victim landscape:

**Who is affected?**  
Women and girls represent the majority of reported victims.

**Where are victims being reported?**  
The highest reported figures are concentrated in specific countries and regions.

**How are patterns changing?**  
Victim numbers fluctuate over time, with a significant increase around 2018–2019.

Together, these insights show how interactive data visualization can make complex humanitarian data easier to explore and provide a stronger foundation for evidence-based decision-making.
